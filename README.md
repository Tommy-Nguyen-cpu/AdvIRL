## Abstract
The increasing deployment of AI models in critical applications has exposed them to significant risks from adversarial attacks. While adversarial vulnerabilities in 2D vision models have been extensively studied, the impact of adversarial attacks on 3D models, particularly generative models like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), remains largely unexplored. In this work, we highlight the potential danger posed by adversarial noise embedded in NeRF models, which can manifest in real-world settings and compromise the performance of vision systems such as autonomous vehicles and security cameras. To address this threat, we propose \textit{AdvIRL}, a novel framework that generates adversarial NeRF models by leveraging Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (Instant-NGP) and Reinforcement Learning. Our approach creates adversarial noise robust to diverse real-world transformations, allowing it to effectively execute black-box attacks across various conditions. Furthermore, the adversarial NeRF models generated by \textit{AdvIRL} can be utilized as adversarial training data to improve the robustness of vision models against such attacks. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework across diverse scenes, ranging from small objects like bananas to large-scale environments such as lighthouses, showcasing its broad applicability. Two particular scenes that demonstrated superior results were the banana scene and truck scene. The confidences ranged from 35\% to 75\% for the banana scene and 15\% to 70\% for the truck scene across the majority of observed transformations. Other experiments followed suit with high confidences and robustness.
