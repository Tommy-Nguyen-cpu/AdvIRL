Abstract

The proliferation of AI models across various domains has brought attention to the vulnerabilities posed by adversarial attacks. In response, the emerging field of adversarial architecture explores how adversarial noise influences architectural design and perception. In this thesis, we present a novel reinforcement learning framework leveraging an image segmentation algorithm and a Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) model to generate adversarial noise resilient to diverse transformations. Our investigation reveals that a grid-based NeRF model surpasses NeRF-- in both performance and rendering quality. While NeRF-- requires 2 hours to render a forward-facing scene with significant artifacts, the grid-based NeRF model accomplishes the task in just 20 seconds with minimal artifacts. Consequently, we integrate the grid-based NeRF model into our reinforcement learning pipeline. Additionally, we compare two approaches for adversarial noise generation: modifying NeRF input images and altering NeRF parameters. We observe that modifying NeRF parameters yields superior results due to its lower dimensionality and greater parameter exploration capabilities. Furthermore, NeRFs are recognized for their tendency to smooth out details in scenes. Consequently, imperceptible noise introduced in the input images is highly likely to be smoothed out during the rendering process. Moreover, our analysis revealed that integrating an image segmentation algorithm such as Detectron2, coupled with a well-designed reward system encompassing both rewards and penalties, empowers the agent to selectively modify the texture of the object within the scene. Overall, our study demonstrates the effectiveness of our pipeline in untargeted and targeted scenarios. For instance, our agent successfully generates adversarial noise for a banana, with 130 out of 175 transformations correctly classified as the target class "slug". Similarly, in the lighthouse scene, 13 out of 20 images are misclassified as a "boathouse". Although the truck scene fails to target a specific class, it effectively reduces the classifier's confidence in identifying the truck as a "pickup truck" across various angles. While demonstrating commendable performance, there remains ample room for enhancement, particularly in optimizing memory usage and leveraging specialized image segmentation algorithms tailored for architectural objects. These avenues present promising directions for future research endeavors.
